# Timeline of the Cane-Wallace Criminal Enterprise

**From the Tele-Lawyer/Dynamic Associates Merger to the Davi Skin Pump & Dump**

---

## Overview of the Alleged Enterprise

The following timeline documents the formation and operation of what court filings describe as a criminal enterprise consisting of Kyleen E. Cane (formerly Michael A. Cane), Jan Wallace, and their associates.[^1] The enterprise allegedly operated within the environment of business start-up funding, public shells, and reverse mergers, using Cane's position as a securities attorney to provide legal credibility, while Wallace provided corporate insider access as serial CEO of public shell companies.[^2]

The scheme followed a repeatable pattern: (1) Cane and Wallace formed or acquired shell entities and blank-check companies; (2) reverse merger transactions were used to move assets between entities while diluting outside shareholders; (3) Cane concealed beneficial ownership through use of her former male identity and CEDE Depository Trust accounts; (4) Wallace concealed ownership through offshore nominee accounts at LOM Securities in Bermuda; and (5) the entities were pumped and dumped or stripped of value through self-dealing transactions.[^3]

### The Enterprise Playbook

The enterprise employed a repeatable ten-step methodology across four public shell companies (Dynamic Associates, MW Medical, SDI Industries, and Davi Skin):

1. **Entry via debt**: Wallace extended personal loans to create default leverage.
2. **Install accounting team**: Johal or Sim placed as CFO for controlled financial reporting.
3. **Create manufactured conflict**: Lawsuits and management disputes generated pretext for intervention.
4. **Use conflict to distract**: Ongoing litigation diverted attention from asset extraction.
5. **Control audit committee**: A single-member audit committee provided rubber-stamp oversight.
6. **Install Wallace as CEO**: Wallace assumed executive authority during fabricated "crises."
7. **Liquidate assets and extract value**: Self-dealing transactions stripped corporate value under the guise of turnaround management.
8. **Execute reverse split**: Outstanding shares consolidated to wipe out outside shareholders.
9. **Sell shell or collapse into bankruptcy**: The depleted entity was recycled via reverse merger or abandoned.
10. **Move to next target**: The enterprise pivoted to a new shell, recycling the same co-conspirators.

This playbook was replicated with minor variations across each entity, as documented in the timeline below and the Cross-Entity Pattern Analysis (Section VII).[^76]

## Phase I: Genesis of the Enterprise (1989--2000)

### May 1, 1989 -- Tele-Lawyer Inc. Founded

Michael A. Cane incorporates Tele-Lawyer, Inc. in Nevada as a private legal services and technology company. Cane serves as President, CEO, and Director from inception.[^4]

### July 20, 1989 -- Dynamic Associates Inc. Incorporated

Dynamic Associates, Inc. (CIK 0000878146) is incorporated in Nevada as a holding company for venture businesses. Jan Wallace serves as President, CEO, and Director. The company is classified under SIC Code 6770 (Blank Checks).[^5]

### 1996 -- Dynamic Associates Acquisition Spree

Dynamic Associates acquires multiple healthcare entities to establish an operating base:

- **April 23, 1996**: Acquires 50% of P\&H Laboratories for \$1,000,000 cash.[^6]
- **August 1, 1996**: Acquires 100% of Genesis Health Management Corporation for \$15,000,000 (\$12M cash, \$3M note, 3M shares).[^7]
- **March 13, 1997**: Acquires Geriatric Care Centers of America for \$500,000 cash plus 150,000 shares.[^8]

### December 4, 1997 -- MW Medical Inc. Incorporated

MW Medical, Inc. (CIK 0001059577) is incorporated in Nevada. Wallace serves as President, CEO, and Director. Grace Sim serves as CFO.[^9]

### February 25--26, 1998 -- MW Medical Spun Off from Dynamic Associates

Dynamic Associates distributes MW Medical as a separate public company via 1:1 share distribution to Dynamic shareholders. MW Medical receives P\&H Laboratories, Microwave Medical Corp., and \$2,170,000 in shareholder loans.[^10] Wallace orchestrates the spin-off while simultaneously serving as Director, President, and COO of Dynamic Associates.[^11]

### November 1998 -- Cane Becomes Dynamic's Attorney

Michael A. Cane begins representing Dynamic Associates as outside counsel, "handling mostly business and securities matters for Dynamic."[^12] This dual role---serving as both Dynamic's securities attorney and the founder/CEO of a private company (Tele-Lawyer) that would soon merge with Dynamic---creates a conflict of interest that the proxy statement itself acknowledges.[^13]

### 1999--2000 -- Merger Negotiations

Dynamic's management, led by Wallace, begins "soliciting and negotiating various business opportunities" in 1999.[^14] In January 2000, Wallace contacts Cane---Dynamic's own attorney---about a possible merger with Tele-Lawyer.[^15] The proxy filing states Wallace "obtained outside counsel to assist her in the negotiations regarding any potential merger with Tele-Lawyer" to avoid conflicts of interest,[^16] but Cane simultaneously represented Dynamic as securities counsel and owned 100% of the entity to be acquired.

### November 28, 2000 -- Agreement and Plan of Merger Executed

Dynamic Associates and Tele-Lawyer execute the Agreement and Plan of Merger, calling for the merger of Tele-Lawyer into Dynamic.[^17]

## Phase II: The Reverse Merger & Identity Concealment (2001)

### June 12, 2001 -- Reverse Merger Consummated

The merger is completed. The critical terms:

- Dynamic Associates is renamed **Legal Access Technologies, Inc.** (ticker: LGAL).[^18]
- A **153:1 reverse stock split** dilutes existing Dynamic shareholders by **99.3%**.[^19]
- Virtually all of Dynamic's \$8.4 million in debt is converted to equity.[^20]
- Cane acquires **48.7% beneficial ownership** (2,871,051 shares) of the merged entity.[^21]
- All Dynamic officers and directors resign; Tele-Lawyer's officers and directors replace them.[^22]
- Wallace resigns as officer and director and purportedly "sold all of her shares in the company."[^23]

The FBI's 302 report records Cane's own description[^24]:

\begin{quote}
Telelawyer went through a `reverse merger' with Dynamic Associates, which was a public company. After the reverse merger CANE became a director. CANE defined reverse merger as `when a private company takes over a public company.'
\end{quote}

### June 26, 2001 -- SC 13D Filed: Signed as "Michael A. Cane"

Cane files a Schedule 13D with the SEC disclosing beneficial ownership of Legal Access Technologies stock. The filing is signed **"Michael A. Cane"**---two days before the legal name change.[^25]

### June 28, 2001 -- Legal Name Change: Michael A. Cane to Kyleen E. Cane

Cane's legal name change from Michael A. Cane to Kyleen E. Cane becomes effective. This occurs precisely **sixteen days** after the reverse merger.[^26] The timing is significant: the name change effectively severs the paper trail connecting the new majority shareholder "Kyleen Cane" to "Michael Cane," the attorney who had negotiated the merger on behalf of both parties.

### July 2, 2001 -- SC 13D/A Amendment Filed: Signed as "Michael A. Cane"

Four days after the legal name change, Cane files an amendment to the Schedule 13D with the SEC for Legal Access Technologies. The filing is signed **"Michael A. Cane"** as "Beneficial Owner."[^72] This is the first SEC filing executed under the defunct legal name after the name change became effective.

### July 5, 2001 -- Cane Appointed Director of Las Vegas Gaming as "Michael Cane"

Despite the legal name change seven days prior, Cane is appointed director of Las Vegas Gaming, Inc. under the name "Michael Cane."[^27] The biographical statement accompanying the appointment references Cane's role as "President and Chief Executive Officer of Tele-Lawyer" and "Chairman of the Board and CEO of Legal Access Technologies."[^28]

## Phase III: MW Medical Bankruptcy, Shell Recycling & Continued Identity Fraud (2002--2004)

### January 22, 2002 -- MW Medical Files Chapter 11 Bankruptcy

MW Medical, Inc. files for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court, District of Arizona. Wallace is the **sole secured creditor**, holding a security interest in all of MW Medical's assets---"patented and proprietary technology, ongoing product approvals, inventory and goodwill."[^29]

### February 4, 2002 -- Joint Plan of Reorganization Filed

The Joint Plan of Reorganization is filed in MW Medical's bankruptcy. The plan is designed to allow Wallace, as sole secured creditor, to acquire controlling equity in the reorganized debtor.[^30]

### July 31, 2002 -- Cane Signs MW Fitness Articles of Incorporation as "Michael Cane"

More than one year after her legal name change, Cane signs the Articles of Incorporation for MW Fitness, Inc. (an MW Medical subsidiary) as **"Michael Cane."**[^31]

### October 2, 2002 -- Cane Signs Certificate of Amendment for Savannah Corp as "Michael Cane"

Cane signs the Certificate of Amendment changing the name of MW Fitness Inc. to Savannah Corp., again using the name **"Michael Cane"**---fifteen months after the legal name change.[^32]

### March 26, 2003 -- Wallace Acquires 74,000,000 Shares of MW Medical

As sole secured creditor, Wallace converts \$375,000 of her promissory note into **74,000,000 shares** of MW Medical common stock at **\$0.005 per share**, leaving a remaining note balance of \$570,775.30.[^33] This transaction gives Wallace overwhelming control of MW Medical's equity, obtained through a bankruptcy process in which Cane served as securities counsel.

### November 14, 2003 -- SOX Certification Signed as "Michael A. Cane"

Cane signs the Sarbanes-Oxley Act Section 302/906 certification for Las Vegas Gaming, Inc.'s Form 10-Q (Q3 2003) under the name **"Michael A. Cane"**---two years and five months after the legal name change.[^34] SOX certifications carry criminal penalties for false statements under 18 U.S.C. sections 1350.

### April 14, 2004 -- SOX Certification Signed as "Michael A. Cane"

Cane signs the SOX certification for Las Vegas Gaming's Form 10-K (FY 2003) as **"Michael A. Cane."**[^35]

### May 17, 2004 -- SOX Certification Signed as "Michael A. Cane"

Cane signs the SOX certification for Las Vegas Gaming's Form 10-Q (Q1 2004) as **"Michael A. Cane."**[^36]

### June 24, 2004 -- MW Medical Emerges from Bankruptcy as Davi Skin Inc.

MW Medical, Inc. completes its reorganization and is renamed **Davi Skin, Inc.** (ticker: DAVN, CIK 0001059577). Wallace's remaining promissory note balance of \$570,775.30 remains convertible into common stock.[^37]

### July 22, 2004 -- First SEC Disclosure of Name Change (3+ Year Gap)

Cane files a **Form 5** (Annual Statement of Changes in Beneficial Ownership) that for the first time discloses the name change from Michael A. Cane to Kyleen E. Cane to the SEC.[^38] This disclosure comes **three years and twenty-four days** after the June 28, 2001 legal name change. During the intervening period, Cane signed multiple SEC filings, SOX certifications, corporate documents, and court filings under the legally defunct name "Michael A. Cane."

### August 1, 2004 -- Davi Skin Board Reconstituted

Following the MW Medical/Davi Skin transaction, Wallace and Grace Sim resign. A new board is formed comprising Davi Skin founders Carlo Mondavi (Chairman), Parrish Medley, and Josh Levine.[^39] Wallace assigns her \$200,000 promissory note against MW Medical to Parrish Medley as part of the transaction.[^40] Cane serves as securities attorney handling the transaction.[^41]

## Phase IV: Shell Company Disposal & Offshore Structuring (2004--2007)

### November 18, 2004 -- Legal Access Technologies Spins Off Subsidiaries

Legal Access Technologies spins off its two operating subsidiaries---Tele-Lawyer, Inc. and Perspectives Health Management Corp.---followed by a 30:1 reverse stock split. 208,291 shares are distributed 1:1, leaving LATI as an empty public shell.[^42]

### January 11, 2005 -- Wallace Pays LOM Securities Nominee Renewal Fees

Wallace pays annual renewal fees of \$2,500 each for her four offshore nominee accounts at LOM Securities in Bermuda and the Cayman Islands: **Arch Ltd.**, **Hepburn Holdings Ltd.**, **Sunshine Ltd.**, and **The Chloe Group of Companies** (named after Wallace's daughter, Chloe Cutler).[^43] Funds are wired from Wallace's JP Morgan Chase and HSBC Bank USA accounts to The Bank of Bermuda Limited (account 1010-956504) and Bank of NT Butterfield (Bermuda) (account 20.006.840.351501.100).[^44]

LOM Securities is owned by LOM Holdings, a Bermuda-based financial services group founded by brothers Brian Lines and Scott Lines. The SEC subsequently sued LOM Holdings for engaging in illegal pump-and-dump penny stock schemes.[^45]

### April 27, 2005 -- Legal Access Technologies Acquires World Explorer Corporation

LATI acquires World Explorer Corporation, a company "intending to develop a maritime salvage business," in a further shell recycling transaction. The company's Board also approves a name change from "Legal Access Technologies" to "UnderSea Recovery Corporation."[^46]

### August 30, 2006 -- Legal Access Technologies Voluntarily Deregisters

Legal Access Technologies files **Form 15-12G**, voluntarily deregistering its securities with the SEC and terminating its reporting obligations.[^47]

### April 3, 2007 -- Promissory Note Converts into Offshore Nominee Shares

The Medley promissory note (originally Wallace's \$200,000 note assigned to Parrish Medley) converts into Davi Skin common stock. The resulting **2,295,388 shares** are placed in equal amounts of **573,847 shares** into four offshore brokerage accounts held by nominee companies at LOM Securities in Bermuda and the Cayman Islands: **Arch Ltd.**, **Sunshine Ltd.**, **Chloe Group of Companies**, and **Hepburn Holdings Ltd.**[^48]

### June 3, 2007 -- Wallace Email: "Keep the Facts Hidden"

Wallace emails instructions regarding the offshore accounts, writing: *"I have the instructions. Please go to a fax and call me when you are ready to receive. I moved it to Hepburn Holdings (from ARCH Ltd.) it's been around for a long time, less obvious.. Please keep the facts hidden."*[^49]

## Phase VI: Unraveling & Consequences (2008--2015)

### February 4--7, 2008 -- Thomas & Wong Trial: $1.3 Million Judgment

A jury trial in *Thomas and Wong General Contractor, Inc. v. Blume Law Firm, P.C., Jan Wallace, et al.*, No. CV2005-051325, Maricopa County Superior Court, results in a verdict in favor of Thomas & Wong. Wallace is found liable for **breach of fiduciary duty and abuse of trust** in connection with a confidence scheme involving fake gold and use of Cane's attorney trust account at Cane O'Neill in Paradise Valley, Arizona. Total judgment: **\$1,306,144** against Wallace.[^57]

The Bankruptcy Appellate Panel later ruled this judgment **nondischargeable** under 11 U.S.C. `S` 523(a)(4) (fraud or defalcation while acting in a fiduciary capacity).[^58]

### August 27, 2012 -- Davi Skin Registration Revoked

The SEC revokes Davi Skin's registration for failure to file required periodic reports.[^59]

### November 4, 2013 -- Wallace 341 Bankruptcy Testimony

At her Section 341 Meeting of Creditors in U.S. Bankruptcy Court, District of Nevada, Wallace testifies under oath that she has:

- Served as **CEO of six public companies**
- Filed **three personal bankruptcies**
- Held a **\$1,400,000 claim** in MW Medical[^60]
- Had involvement with **Hepburn Holdings** (Bermuda offshore entity)[^61]
- Connections to **Savannah Corporation** (Nevada)[^62]

### June 17, 2014 -- Federal Indictment: $300 Million Pump-and-Dump Conspiracy

A federal indictment is unsealed in the Eastern District of New York (Brooklyn) charging Cane in connection with a **\$300 million pump-and-dump conspiracy** involving CodeSmart Holdings, Inc. and Cubed, Inc. Co-defendants include Abraxas J. Discala, Justin Wexler, Matthew Bell, and others.[^63]

The indictment alleges that in early May 2013, Discala and co-conspirators engineered a reverse merger of CodeSmart with a shell public company, then on two occasions fraudulently inflated the stock price and sold unrestricted shares at a profit. During the first pump-and-dump (May 13--August 21, 2013), the stock price was manipulated from \$1.77 to \$6.94 (291% increase) before crashing to \$2.19. During the second (August 21--September 20, 2013), the price was raised from \$2.19 to \$4.60 (104% increase) before dropping to \$2.13.[^64]

The shell company used in the CodeSmart reverse merger was prepared using **Cane Clark LLP's EDGARizer license** for SEC filings.[^65]

### July 17, 2014 -- SEC Civil Complaint Filed

The SEC files a civil complaint, *SEC v. Discala et al.*, in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New York, alleging securities fraud, market manipulation, and related violations.[^66]

### May 8, 2015 -- ProPublica: "How a Dubious Super PAC Boosted a Questionable Penny Stock"

ProPublica publishes an investigation connecting Cane to the "Voters for Hillary" Super PAC and CrossClick Media Inc. (XCLK). Cane contributed a \$10,700 loan to the PAC and paid early expenses via personal credit card. CrossClick's stock spiked 12-fold, and its shares expanded from 170 million to 2.6 billion in months.[^67]

### September 2--4, 2015 -- Panama/International Press: FBI Investigation

- **September 2, 2015** -- *La Prensa* (Panama) reports FBI investigation of former Panama President Ricardo Martinelli for transferring \$100,000 to Cane Clark LLP in 2010.[^68]
- **September 3, 2015** -- *La Prensa* characterizes Cane as "under investigation for link to scheme of fraud and manipulation of US stock market."[^69]
- **September 4, 2015** -- *Dominicanos Hoy* reports federal police investigating Cane and six others for artificially inflating share values on U.S. exchanges.[^70]

### November 2015 -- Superseding Indictment Filed

A superseding indictment is filed in the Eastern District of New York expanding the charges against Cane and co-defendants.[^71]

## Section VIII: False Claims Act Nexus

The enterprise touched government programs at three documented points, creating Qui Tam exposure under the False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. sections 3729--3733):

**Claim 1: Genesis/PHMC Medicare Billing (1994--2001).** Genesis Health Management Corporation billed Medicare under "cost plus reimbursement" arrangements across 32 rural hospital units in seven states. Wallace served as CEO overseeing all billing operations. Estimated cumulative Medicare billings: \$50M--\$80M. Each false claim submitted to CMS constitutes a separate FCA violation.[^81]

**Claim 2: SDI/Secured Lending Mortgage Fraud (2006).** Wallace signed a branch agreement with Americash, "an approved Fannie Mae Seller/Servicer." If mortgage applications originated through the SDI/Secured Lending/Americash channel contained false statements about borrower qualifications, property values, or income, each application sold to Fannie Mae constitutes a False Claims Act violation.[^82]

**Claim 3: Galaxy Gaming CARES Act Loans (2020).** Galaxy Gaming, the successor entity that acquired the SDI shell through reverse merger, obtained a \$4,000,000 unsecured loan through Nevada State Bank "under section 13(3) of the Federal Reserve Act" on October 26, 2020. If the loan application contained false statements about the company's history, ownership structure, or financial condition, this creates FCA exposure under the CARES Act provisions.[^83]
